Overview

For periodic collision-free planar target motions with nonincreasing pairwise relative angles, the paper bounds the number of uniquely optimal squared-distance matchings by max(1, 2W), where W is total clockwise pair winding. The same bound holds for a coordinate-lexicographic canonical selection. Rigid rotation has the sharp count n(n − 1).

For densely encoded rational Fourier trajectories, an exact algorithm checks the geometric hypotheses and solves registration without a genericity assumption. Counterexamples separate angular monotonicity from winding alone and show a three-dimensional obstruction. Further results treat specified small mass perturbations and a sharp single-defect transport bound.

Condensed summary. See the manuscript for the full abstract, statements, and proofs.

Scope & limitations

The general count requires angular monotonicity; the effective algorithm requires dense rational Fourier input. Its output covers optimal values, not all tied assignments. General arbitrary-mass transportation and three-dimensional asymptotic counts remain open.
