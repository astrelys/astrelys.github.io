Overview

This note studies three proposed selection mechanisms: maximizing products of principal minors, replacing projection noise by its convex hull, and choosing a basis by Gaussian-target ℓ₁ optimization. Explicit examples quantify the failure of each surrogate principle.

The constructions include global principal-volume maximizers with error ratios tending to three, a strict mixed-noise versus projection-noise gap that survives a full-spark perturbation, and a deterministic frame sequence obstructing subharmonic Gaussian-target cost thresholds. An explicit Fourier reconstruction also produces low-cost coordinate bases in a classical conference-square family.

Condensed summary. See the manuscript for the full abstract, statements, and proofs.

Scope & limitations

These obstructions concern the specified mechanisms. They do not rule out all selection algorithms or resolve unrestricted minimax basis selection.
